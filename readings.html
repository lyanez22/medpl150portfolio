Reflection on Susan Sontag’s On Photography

Photography, to me, is the art of noticing—the composition of objects, the beauty of nature, and the moments that can be captured and preserved. Every photograph carries a message that can be enhanced through editing. Throughout history, photography has been essential in depicting moments we cannot return to: civil rights movements, wars, historical figures, and countless events that would otherwise fade.

Sontag discusses how images can be manipulated to create false narratives. With modern editing tools and AI technologies, this manipulation has only become more powerful. War photography, for example, can be edited to glorify or demonize a side depending on the agenda behind the image. Such alterations lead to misinformation and distorted perceptions of reality.

This is why it’s important for viewers to approach photographs critically, recognizing that images can be both tools of truth and instruments of deception.

Graphic Novel: Motherhood in Crisis

In Sierra Leone, becoming a mother often means entering a life-or-death struggle. Motherhood in Crisis is a powerful graphic novel sharing the experiences of women facing the realities of pregnancy and childbirth in one of the most dangerous places in the world to give birth.

Memunatu - Birth in my village: This story discusses about the life-threatening circumstances that many pregnant women face in Sierra Leone, where medical attention is unreachable, leading to many pregnant women have complicated and life-threatening labor experiences. This story follows Memunatu’s story giving birth at her home as her grandmother helped her throughout the labor process. Tragically, her baby died in her stomach and began experiencing severe symptoms. A group of 9 people came to offer help and took her to get medical treatment at the Aberdeen women’s centre. She met people from different districts and got the surgery she needed and additional treatment to ease the pain. Fast forward to 2020, Memunatu was able to have a baby back the centre. Memunatu’s story depicts the reality many women who live in rural areas go through. Lack of resources, hospitals, and basic necessities can lead to many in the same path as Memunatu’s story. Heela - My stolen future: This story discusses about a spike of sexual assaults towards school girls in Sierra Leone, where many have to make a difficult situation of leaving their education and future aspirations. Through Heela’s story (2020), she learns that she was pregnant and was trying to see if she could get an abortion. Her fears of getting treatment or seeing medical attention was based on how Ebola spread to many, including her own mother, who unfortunately passed away. Heels lives with her grandmother who helped her go to the police station to arrest the man who SAed her and left her pregnant. Heela received counseling and got transferred to the Rainbow centre. Heela was able to process all the mental and physical damage done to her and met others who were going to a similar situation. Heela now has a her own child and was able to reunite with her father. Kadiatu - The Devil at my door: In Kadiatu’s story, we learn about all her children passing away due to “unknown” reasons. Kadiatu went through so much on her last labor experience and her child almost went through the same painful symptoms (high fevers and numbness) Dr Nellie Bell explains that theses symptoms were caused by a disease caused by malaria. Fortunately, Kadiatu’s child, Abdul was saved. Teetteh - My punishment: Teetteh spent three months pregnant in a correctional centre, where many undergo cruel mistreatment like rejecting her treatment or lack of medication. Before entering the correctional centre, she lived with her husband who abused her and even while she was pregnant. This situation turned for the worst when one night, her husband attacked from behind while she was peeling potatoes and defended her self with the knife she was using.
